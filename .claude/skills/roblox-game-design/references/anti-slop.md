# Anti-slop protocol

"Slop" means mechanics imported because *Roblox games usually have them*, not because *this game needs them*. The result is interchangeable experiences with no identity.

## The question
**Why does this system belong in this game?** A valid answer names the **core fantasy or pillar** it serves and the **player decision or feeling** it creates. "Players like it", "it helps retention" and "every simulator has it" are not valid answers on their own.

## Watchlist: common mechanics and the question each must answer
| Mechanic | Must answer |
|---|---|
| Pets / companions | What do they *do* that expresses the fantasy (behavior, not stat bonuses)? |
| Eggs / gacha / loot boxes | Why is randomness better than choice here? Is it paid? (`PolicyService` rules, ethics with young audiences) |
| Rebirth / prestige | What *changes* after reset: new rules, spaces or understanding? Or is it the same loop with bigger numbers? |
| Generic coins / gems / multiple currencies | What distinct decision does each currency create? Where are the sinks? |
| Rarity tiers (common → mythic) | Do tiers differ in *kind* or only in numbers? Are there too many tiers to read? |
| Daily rewards / streaks | Does it bring players back to something worth returning to, or punish absence? |
| Battle pass | Is there enough meaningful content cadence to fill it? |
| Generic shop UI | Could acquisition be diegetic or tied to the world (a merchant, a crafting bench, salvaged parts)? |
| Upgrade-everything stat ladders | Does any upgrade change play, or are they all +%? |
| Leaderboards | Is the ranked metric something skilled or creative play produces, or just time spent? |

## Verdicts
- **Justified:** keep it. Write one line naming the fantasy or pillar and the decision it creates.
- **Transform:** keep the *function* and make it specific. Example: "coins" in a time-travel heist become *time shards* spent to rewind a failed attempt, so the currency is the fantasy. Example: "pets" in a horror game become a dog that hears the monster before you do: its behavior is the gameplay, and it's vulnerable.
- **Remove:** it serves no pillar, and the effort goes to what makes the game distinct.

Present verdicts to the user. They own the final call. Monetization is legitimate; the question is whether the design is coherent and fair, not whether money exists.

## Generic-ness smell test (for any concept or feature)
- Could you swap the theme without changing a single mechanic? Then the mechanics don't express the theme.
- Is the most memorable moment a number going up? Find a moment worth telling a friend about.
- Is there a moment a player would screenshot, clip or retell? If not, design one.
